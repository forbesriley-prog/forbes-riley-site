My story

There was a version of my story meant to end in a hospital waiting room.

My father spent three years in a hospital after an accident. Fifteen operations. My whole high school. He wanted to be an inventor, and he never got to build it. I decided, before I had the words for it, that I would build everything for both of us.

I went to Broadway. I shared a stage with Christopher Reeve and worked for Bob Fosse, eight shows a week, carrying thirty extra pounds under every costume and hiding it. Great on stage. Miserable in the mirror.

Then television. The original X-Games on ESPN. A daily national talk show. Twenty years of live home shopping, where you have ninety seconds to make a stranger reach for a phone. And when I finally built my own product, the experts told me on national television that it would never work.

I sold it out of the back of my car. Then I sold a million of them.

Somewhere in that fight I noticed the same thing kept showing up in every pitch that worked. A pattern. A process. A formula.

I call it the Pitch Secrets formula

Today I speak to entrepreneurs, sales teams, Fortune 100 companies, women's groups and colleges, people who are brilliant at what they do and freeze the second they have to say it out loud.

The formula

The formula behind everything I teach

It wasn't drawn on a whiteboard. It was built on live television with a clock running and a number on the screen. Tap a pillar.

E

Engage

Seven seconds. A hook that earns the next sentence, whether it's a stage, a DM or an elevator.

"Lead with the one line they'd repeat to a friend."
E

Excite

Story before features. The reason before the product. Energy they can feel from the back row.

"If you're not excited, why would they be?"
E

Enroll

The ask, the objections and the close, said with your shoulders back. A YES opens doors.

"You don't get what you deserve. You get what you pitch for."

This is not a motivational formula. It is a selling formula, tested on 180 national campaigns and taught to 125,000 students.
