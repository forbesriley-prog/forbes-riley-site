My story

There was a version of my story meant to end in a hospital waiting room.

My father spent three years in a hospital after an accident. Fifteen operations. My whole high school. He wanted to be an inventor, and he never got to build it. I watched that dream sit in a bed and I decided, before I had the words for it, that I would build everything for both of us.

I went to Broadway. I shared a stage with Christopher Reeve and worked for Bob Fosse, eight shows a week, carrying thirty extra pounds under every costume and hiding it. Great on stage. Miserable in the mirror. For years.

Then television. The original X-Games on ESPN. A daily national talk show. Twenty years of live home shopping, where you have ninety seconds to make a stranger reach for a phone. And when I finally built my own product, SpinGym, the experts told me on national television that it would never work.

I sold it out of the back of my car. Then I sold a million of them.

Somewhere in that fight I noticed the same thing kept showing up, in every pitch that worked and every one that didn't. A pattern. A process. A formula.

Engage. Excite. Enroll.

The three pitch patterns behind $2.5 billion in sales. Not something I read in a book. Something I lived, on live television, with a clock running.

Nobody buys the product. They buy the reason.

The promise

Here's what I promise every room I walk into

I take a stage seriously. When someone gives me their audience, I don't show up to entertain. I show up to put a skill in their hands that is still there when the event is over and the phone rings on Monday.

Outcome 1

A 7-second hook

Every person leaves with an opening line they can say tonight, built in the room, not in a workbook they never open.

Outcome 2

Their WHY in one sentence

Nobody buys the product. They buy the reason. I get each person to say theirs out loud, and I don't move on until it's clear.

Outcome 3

Confidence as posture

Feet. Shoulders. Breath. Then speak. Posture first, feeling second. Confidence you can do on purpose, even on a screen.

Outcome 4

No more sounding salesy

The exact shift from pushing to inviting. Your people stop apologising for the ask and start enrolling.

Outcome 5

Reps before they leave

A pitch is a performance. We rehearse it out loud, live, with coaching, so it belongs to them before the room empties.
