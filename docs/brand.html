05 · Voice, names and bios

Confident, warm, specific. Sell the result, not the feeling.

The Queen of Pitch

Her name and title.
"Dr. Forbes Riley" the first time on a page, "Forbes" after that. Her title is the Queen of Pitch. Retire "Queen of Infomercials" and the stacked job titles ("Celebrity TV Host, Motivational Speaker, High Performance Success & Results Coach"). One title, said once.
Taglines we use.
The Queen of Pitch · The $2.5 Billion Host · Turn every pitch into a YES · Give your audience a skill, not a feeling · Pitch better, earn more · "Tell me more."
How we write.
Short sentences. Numbers, not adjectives. "You" more than "we". Say what they walk out with. One ask per page. Urgency is real (a date, a seat count, a print run), never a fake countdown that resets.
What we do not say.
No "TEDx". No SpinGym in a speaking bio. No "thirty years" at the top of anything for creators. No made-up names on testimonials. No "Queen of Infomercials". No asterisks on the $2.5 billion.

One line

Dr. Forbes Riley, the Queen of Pitch: the host behind $2.5 billion in live-TV sales, USA TODAY bestselling author of Pitch Secrets A to Z, and the keynote speaker who gives your audience a skill, not a feeling.

Short · 60 words

Dr. Forbes Riley is a global expert in communication, sales and performance. Known as the Queen of Pitch, she has generated $2.5 billion in sales, reached more than 80 countries on live TV and hosted two national talk shows. A USA TODAY bestselling author, TV host and award-winning coach, she teaches entrepreneurs, sales teams and speakers to get to YES.

Long · 110 words

Dr. Forbes Riley started on Broadway, then spent three decades hosting on the biggest live shopping networks in the world, where she sold more than $2.5 billion of products with no second take. Live TV taught her that you have seconds to earn attention, so she broke the pitch down into patterns anyone can learn. Known as the Queen of Pitch, she has hosted two national talk shows and ESPN's X-Games, reached more than 80 countries, and been named to the National Fitness Hall of Fame. Her USA TODAY bestseller, Pitch Secrets A to Z, and her masterclass have taught tens of thousands of entrepreneurs, sales teams and speakers to get to YES.

07 · The story

The beats we tell, in this order

Every "about" and every origin video uses these. Pick three for a short page, all of them for a long one. Nothing else goes in without Forbes signing off.

  1. Long Island, a teenager. Her dad, an inventor, lost part of his hand in an accident. Bills piled up.
  2. At 16 she entered a pageant for the scholarship, not the crown. She won. "You can change your life, no matter where you start."
  3. Hollywood said no, again and again. She kept going. Broadway first.
  4. Live TV. Thirty years hosting on the biggest live shopping networks in the world, with no second take. More than $2.5 billion of products sold.
  5. Jack LaLanne, Montel, Fit-TV, X-Games, two talk shows. The names and rooms that prove it (see Facts for the numbers we use).
  6. She broke the pitch down into patterns anyone can learn. The 7-Second Hook, the 3 Pitch Patterns, the masterclass, the book.
  7. The promise. "I don't want your audience to feel inspired. I want them to leave with something they can do."

11 · Page recipes

How a Forbes page is built

Same bones every time, so a visitor who has seen one page knows how to read the next. The book page is the reference build.

Sales page

  1. Urgency bar (real date or stock)
  2. Ribbon: USA TODAY bestseller / the proof line
  3. H1 promise + one sub line
  4. Video or 3D product left, order card right
  5. Featured-on logos
  6. "Does this sound familiar?" problem
  7. Industry leaders (3)
  8. Proof: readers, rooms, #MadeASale
  9. What's inside
  10. Bonus stack with total value
  11. Hi, I'm Forbes
  12. Vertical video testimonials
  13. FAQ
  14. Sticky buy button, exit pop-up

Webinar / masterclass page

  1. Date, time, "live on Zoom", one register button
  2. H1: what they will master, not "the secret"
  3. Forbes cutout, red or gold, on black
  4. Featured-on logos
  5. What you'll master (6 bullets, her vocabulary)
  6. Hot seat coaching promise
  7. 3 named testimonials
  8. Who it's for
  9. Register again, same button, same words

Link in bio

  1. Signature logo, gold
  2. "The Queen of Pitch" + "$2.5 billion in live-TV sales"
  3. At most 6 buttons, gold, in ladder order: masterclass, book, speaking, podcast, EP 2.0, challenge
  4. Social icons
  5. No personal email on the page

Speaker page

  1. "Now booking" bar
  2. H1: a skill, not a feeling
  3. Reel left, availability form right
  4. Featured-on logos
  5. Leaders who have shared her stage
  6. Four signature keynotes
  7. What the room walks out with + formats
  8. Rooms and crowd photos
  9. Attendee videos, booker FAQ, bio

What the current landing pages get wrong, and what ours do instead

Current pages (pitchsecretstraining.com, pitchsecretsbook.com, forbesriley.com)

  • Five or more typefaces on one page (Montserrat, Poppins, Inter, Roboto, Oswald, DM Serif, Open Sans)
  • Red, lime green, yellow, orange, navy and bright blue buttons on the same page
  • A broken video player at the top of the Elevator Pitch page
  • "Two TEDx talks" and "Queen of Infomercials" still in the copy
  • 80,000 students on one page, 45,743 on the next
  • Asterisks on the $2.5 billion, stacked job titles, a personal Gmail on the link page
  • Fake countdowns that reset, "rumours are true" headers

Ours

  • Poppins only, 800 and 400, Allura for one flourish
  • Gold on black, one button colour, text on gold is ink
  • A video that plays, with a poster frame, in the hero
  • Bios from this page, numbers from the Facts board
  • One count for students, everywhere
  • No asterisks, one title, business emails only
  • Real dates, real stock, real names

13 · Rules of the house

Ten things that keep it looking like us

  • Gold on black, white type
  • One hero, one ask, one button colour
  • Real proof: readers, rooms, names with titles and organisations
  • Fill the row. No blank space, no half-empty grids
  • Phone first. Every page checked at phone width before it ships
  • Red or green as design colours (Book Red on book pages is the one exception)
  • Spinning seals, badges over text, giant medals
  • Emoji where we have graphics
  • Fake urgency, fake names, fake counters
  • TEDx, SpinGym or "thirty years" in speaker and creator copy
