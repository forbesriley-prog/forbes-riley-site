04 · Photos

Cut her out. Put her on the stage.

Forbes is always a cutout on black with a gold glow behind her, never a framed studio photo in a box. Red, gold, white and black outfits work. Big expressions, arms up, pointing, mid-laugh. Crowd and stage shots for proof.

  • Cutout on black, soft gold radial glow behind her
  • Red, gold, white or black outfits
  • Stage, crowd and readers-holding-the-book shots as proof
  • Real event photos, real readers, real names
  • Framed studio portraits in a white box
  • Blue dress, plum or busy coloured backgrounds
  • Stock photos, stock people, stock hands
  • Emoji in place of real graphics

05 · Voice, taglines and bios

Confident, warm, specific. Sell the result, not the feeling.

The Queen of Pitch

Taglines we use.
The Queen of Pitch · The $2.5 Billion Host · Turn every pitch into a YES · Give your audience a skill, not a feeling · Pitch Secrets A to Z
How we write.
Short sentences. Numbers, not adjectives. "You" more than "we". Say what the room walks out with. One ask per page. Urgency is real (dates, seats), never fake.
What we do not say.
No "TEDx". No SpinGym in a speaking bio. No "thirty years" at the top of anything for creators. No made-up names on testimonials.

One line

Dr. Forbes Riley, the Queen of Pitch: the host behind $2.5 billion in live-TV sales, USA TODAY bestselling author of Pitch Secrets A to Z, and the keynote speaker who gives your audience a skill, not a feeling.

07 · Rules of the house

Ten things that keep it looking like us

  • Gold on black, white type
  • One hero, one ask, one button colour
  • Real proof: readers, rooms, names with titles and organisations
  • Fill the row. No blank space, no half-empty grids
  • Phone first. Every page checked at phone width before it ships
  • Red or green anywhere in the design
  • Spinning seals, badges over text, giant medals
  • Emoji where we have graphics
  • Fake urgency, fake names, fake counters
  • TEDx, SpinGym or "thirty years" in speaker and creator copy
