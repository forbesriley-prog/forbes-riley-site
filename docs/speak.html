My story

There was a version of my story meant to end in a hospital waiting room.

My father spent three years in a hospital after an accident. Fifteen operations. My whole high school. He wanted to be an inventor, and he never got to build it. I decided, before I had the words for it, that I would build everything for both of us.

I went to Broadway. I shared a stage with Christopher Reeve and worked for Bob Fosse, eight shows a week, carrying thirty extra pounds under every costume and hiding it. Great on stage. Miserable in the mirror.

Then television. The original X-Games on ESPN. A daily national talk show. Twenty years of live home shopping, where you have ninety seconds to make a stranger reach for a phone. And when I finally built my own product, the experts told me on national television that it would never work.

I sold it out of the back of my car. Then I sold a million of them.

Somewhere in that fight I noticed the same thing kept showing up in every pitch that worked. A pattern. A process. A skill anyone can learn.

Time. A yes in seconds instead of a maybe after six meetings. Shorter sales cycles, faster buy-in, less chasing.

Confidence. They walk into any room, any camera, any stage and own it. Not hype: the calm that comes from knowing exactly what to say.
